\subsubsection{Cluster Quality}

Silhouette scores, for the five categories with a valid balanced split, range from \MetricZeroNinecEnhancedSubclassDiscoveryCavityQuenchBreakdownSilhouette{} (\textit{Cavity quench/breakdown}) to \MetricZeroNinecEnhancedSubclassDiscoveryRfSafetyThresholdExceededSilhouette{} (\textit{RF safety threshold exceeded}) -- by the standard scale~\cite{silhouette_interpretation}, four of the five fall below the threshold usually read as "no substantial structure," with only \textit{RF safety threshold exceeded} clearing into the "weak structure" band (\textit{Vacuum threshold}, at \MetricZeroNinecEnhancedSubclassDiscoveryVacuumThresholdSilhouette, does not). We report this range as evidence of a genuine but weakly-separated bimodal substructure, not a well-isolated multi-cluster picture; a higher silhouette would be required to claim the latter.

For the sixth category with no accepted split, a direct follow-up investigation (unconstrained search, same $k=2$--6 sweep, balance constraint relaxed) found three independent methods (K-Means, Ward-linkage and complete-linkage Agglomerative) converging unprompted on the same $\sim$13\% ($\sim$101--103 of 790 events) minority subgroup, silhouette $\approx$0.22 -- a score that would have exceeded three of the five categories that did clear the balance floor. % MANUAL: follow-up investigation run directly against cooked_data_v7 (one-off, not yet a results-manifest entry); "~101-103", "silhouette ~0.22", and the three-of-five comparison are read directly off that run's output, not a separate manifest metric
This is evidence of genuine, cross-method-agreed structure -- not the single-outlier degenerate split (789-vs-1) the unconstrained search's own top pick actually was, exactly the failure mode the balance constraint exists to reject -- that simply falls a few points under a floor chosen as a round number rather than derived from this category's own distribution. We read this as "real structure, floor slightly too strict for this one category," not "no structure," and report it as an open methodological refinement rather than resolving it by loosening the floor after the fact.

Fig.~\ref{fig:phase09_profiles} (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}) shows the top differentiating features per category between the two corrected clusters (Cohen's $d$ effect size) -- a first step toward a systematic per-subtype physical characterization, though not yet a complete one: attributing a specific subtype to a specific physical mechanism (e.g. microphonics vs. an amplifier trip) would require pairing this with targeted expert review, which is future work (Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Weakness Diagnosis}
\label{sec:weakness_diagnosis}

A dedicated diagnostic pass quantifies three sources of weakness raised qualitatively elsewhere in this section: class imbalance, a second independent confusion-matrix cross-check, and whether each supervised task is data-limited or already saturated.

\textbf{Imbalance really does hurt performance}, not just plausibly: Pearson $r$ between category size and mean F1 (averaged across the four multi-label models of Sec.~\ref{sec:multilabel_2x2_results}) is \MetricOneZeroPhaseThreeWeaknessDiagnosisImbalancePearsonR{}, rising to \MetricOneZeroPhaseThreeWeaknessDiagnosisImbalancePearsonRLog{} against $\log_{10}(\text{size})$ -- closer to a logarithmic than a linear relationship, consistent with \textit{Rég signal RF hors tolérance} (\MetricZeroZeroDataAttritionChainGtPassingRGSignalRfHorsTolRance{} events, mean F1 0.935) and \textit{Coupure externe rapide} (\MetricZeroZeroDataAttritionChainGtPassingCoupureExterneRapide{} events, mean F1 0.049) % MANUAL: the two per-category mean-F1 values are read from this manifest's meta.imbalance_per_category, not separately exposed as metrics
sitting at opposite ends. A second, independently-built root-cause confusion matrix (plain unweighted Random Forest, not the class-weighted one of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) reproduces the same qualitative pattern found there on an independent split: \textit{Rég signal RF hors tolérance} events are the dominant source of confusion in both directions, primarily misrouted as \textit{Absence autorisation RF} -- consistent, not identical, cross-validation of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}'s own confusion analysis.

\textbf{Real subtype structure exists within \textit{Rég signal RF hors tolérance} -- unsupervised clustering (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) simply could not discover it without labels.} This dataset separately carries a rule-based 9-way physics subtype label for this category (decoded from the ALM bits: \textit{Fausse alerte}, \textit{Gain chaîne de puissance}, \textit{Oscillation Ucav}, \textit{Perte faisceau}, \textit{Quench}, \textit{Quench adjacent}, \textit{Anomalie de la présence faisceau}, \textit{Erreur de contrôle d'accord}, \textit{Autre}), not discovered by any clustering search. A supervised Random Forest predicting these 9 labels from features reaches macro-F1 $=0.812$ on all \MetricZeroZeroDataAttritionChainGtPassingRGSignalRfHorsTolRance{} events of this category % MANUAL: n_subtypes=9 and macro_f1=0.812 are read from this manifest's meta.confusion.physics_subtype, not separately exposed as metrics
-- a strong, confident result despite the smallest subtype having a single test-set event. This directly resolves the open question left by Sec.~\ref{sec:results}'s subtype-clustering discussion: the absence of a valid \emph{unsupervised} split for this category does not mean no real subtype structure exists, only that unsupervised geometry cannot discover named physics categories it was never given labels for.

\textbf{Learning curves distinguish a data-limited task from an already-saturated one.} 5-fold cross-validated macro-F1 vs.\ training-set fraction (10\%--100\%, 8 points) shows opposite behavior for the two supervised tasks benchmarked in this way: binary classification (Sec.~\ref{sec:binary_ablation_results}) is already at ceiling with just 10\% of its training data (CV macro-F1 $\approx$0.999) and gains essentially nothing from the rest (final train-CV gap \MetricOneZeroPhaseThreeWeaknessDiagnosisBinaryLearningCurveFinalGap{}) -- more data would not move this number. Root cause identification (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) is the opposite: CV macro-F1 climbs from 0.228 at 10\% of its training data to 0.693 at 100\%, % MANUAL: the two curve endpoint values are read from this manifest's meta.learning_curves.root_cause, not separately exposed as metrics
still rising, not flattened, at the end of the tested range (slope \MetricOneZeroPhaseThreeWeaknessDiagnosisRootCauseLearningCurveCvSlope{} over the full range, final train-CV gap \MetricOneZeroPhaseThreeWeaknessDiagnosisRootCauseLearningCurveFinalGap{}) -- a genuinely data-limited task, unlike binary classification, and a concrete argument that more labeled fault events, not a different model, is the more promising lever for improving this specific number further.

\subsection{Early Fault-Onset Detection Analysis}

The corrected precursor model (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology} -- trained only on a fixed pre-trigger window, excluding whole-signal and unbounded steady-state features that were found to confound the task) reaches \MetricZeroFivePhaseZeroPrecursorRandomForestRocAuc{} ROC AUC, against \MetricZeroFivePrecursorWindowLeakInvestigationTruePrecursorIsolationForestAuc{} for a genuinely unsupervised Isolation Forest baseline on the same features -- the gap between the two measures how much of this task's separability requires labels versus how much is detectable as a generic anomaly.

We attempted to measure the detection lead time directly: for \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeNEventsTotal{} sampled true-positive fault events, we tested how far before each event's own trigger the model's predicted probability remained above 0.5, % MANUAL: standard binary decision threshold, not a manifest metric
sliding the same fixed-duration window progressively earlier. The result is predominantly right-censored: \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeCensoredFraction{} of sampled events remained confidently detected all the way to the edge of the tested/available signal buffer (a lower bound of \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeLeadTimeMsLowerBoundMeanCensored{}ms, mean), so no upper bound on the lead time can currently be established for those events. Exactly one of the \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeNEventsTotal{} sampled events showed a genuine, bounded drop below 0.5 within the tested buffer, at \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeLeadTimeMsMeanUncensored{}ms -- too small a sample to characterize a genuine boundary from, but it demonstrates the sliding-window probe can observe one when the buffer permits it, rather than being censored by construction. We read the dominant censored result as more than an incomplete measurement: the \MetricZeroFivePrecursorLeadTimeLeadTimeMsLowerBoundMeanCensored{}ms lower bound is roughly 14$\times$ the 34\,ms window (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}) % MANUAL: 477/34 ratio computed from the two already-cited figures in this paragraph/Sec. 4, not a separate manifest metric
the classifier was actually trained to reason about -- a genuine, temporally-local precursor signal confined to that window would not be expected to remain confidently detectable this far outside it. This is evidence against, not merely inconclusive about, the "genuine near-trigger precursor" reading, and in favor of the alternative explored further below: that at least part of the observed separability reflects a stable per-capture signal characteristic rather than a fault-onset-proximate one (Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}). Directly testing that alternative -- scoring the same restricted feature set on a window drawn from the middle of a Normal event's recording, far from any acquisition boundary -- is immediate follow-up work, not yet done here.
