Does the heat-load observer see a fault coming?

Six neural-network heat-load models — dense, CNN, LSTM, LSTM+attention, and two meta-learner stacks, trained independently per cryomodule on 2022 thermal-balance data — folded into the existing EPICS precursor methodology as six new candidate channels, one per architecture. Same matched-pair distance, temporal-block classifier, VAE latent space, and LSTM-attention methods, same 31 (fault type × scope) combinations, run again with these six channels added to the original 23. Why sensitivity, not magnitude. CMA02 and CMA11 are known to run genuinely higher heat loads than the rest of the fleet — real physical differences, not a modeling artifact. This study isn't chasing an absolute-Watts calibration (separate future work); it asks whether the relative movement of each architecture's output in the hours before a fault differs from its movement before normal operation.
The existing archived channel OBSCHARGE is a different, unrelated heat-load estimate already in the base 23-channel ensemble — the two are never combined or confused here.

Method and honesty notes
  • All 6 models are per-cryomodule (trained separately for each of CMA02–12) and only exist for those 11 modules — every statistic on this page excludes CMA01 and the whole CMB side for the heat-load channels specifically; the other 23 channels retain their original full-fleet coverage.
  • The classical per-channel result is the strongest evidence here, and it is a single-method finding for its two fault types — VAE has no per-channel view to confirm or deny it, the joint LSTM model doesn't rank a heat-load channel in its top-10 for either, and curation surfaces heat-load channels for a different pair of fault types (quench, RF-safety-threshold) where the classical method has no data at all (too few matched pairs). Read this as real-but-single-method evidence, not the multi-method corroboration the base study found for e.g. "RF authorization missing."
  • Same small-n caution as the base study: classifier AUCs from slices with n<30 can swing to extreme values from block-CV instability alone — flagged with ⚠.
  • 759 events had usable heat-load observer output; 623 overlap the base study's in-era matching population (the remainder fall just outside its 2021-06–2024-10 window).
Glossary
Heat-load observer
A neural network trained to estimate a cryomodule's real-time thermal dissipation from cryogenic sensor readings (temperatures, valve positions, pressure, level) — a virtual sensor, not a direct measurement. Six independently-trained architectures are compared here.
AUC (area under the ROC curve)
How well a classifier separates fault from Normal, 0 to 1. 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect. Treat anything near 1.0 from a small slice with suspicion (see the small-n caution), not extra confidence.
Matched-pair median
For each fault event, we found a Normal event from the same (or side-matched) cryomodule close in time, and took the fault event's anomaly-distance score minus its matched partner's. Positive = the fault event looked more unusual at the same point in time; negative = the opposite.
Wilcoxon p / Bonferroni correction
Wilcoxon tests whether matched-pair differences are consistently on one side of zero. Bonferroni tightens the bar in proportion to how many channels are tested at once (29 here), so a channel marked ** cleared that stricter bar; a single * passed the ordinary p<0.05 test but not the corrected one.
Fraction positive
Of all matched pairs, the fraction where the fault event scored higher than its Normal partner. 0.5 is a coin flip; consistently far from 0.5 is what makes a signal believable even when the raw magnitude test is noisy.
Temporal-block cross-validation
Data split into contiguous date chunks; the classifier trains on all but one and is scored on the one it never saw, rotating through every chunk once — prevents it from learning "which era" instead of the actual fault physics.
VAE (variational autoencoder) latent space
A nonlinear alternative to the classical linear (PCA) method: a small neural network compresses each 1-hour window's full channel ensemble to 8 numbers and reconstructs it back. A downstream classifier on those 8 numbers gives an independent AUC per window.
LSTM with attention
Sees the full ordered 16-step sequence across the whole 4h and learns which timesteps to weight most heavily. The chart shows fault attention minus Normal attention at each timestep.
Permutation importance
Shuffle one channel's values across all events and measure how much the trained model's AUC drops — a bigger drop means that channel mattered more to that model.
Curated model
A smaller model retrained on only the top-8 channels by permutation importance, testing whether the full ensemble was mostly carrying dead weight for that fault type.
